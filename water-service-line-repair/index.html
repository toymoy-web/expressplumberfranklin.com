Water Service Line Repair in Franklin, TN

The water service line is the pipe running from your meter to your house, and when it leaks, the signs are specific: a soggy or unusually green patch of yard between the meter and the home, a water bill that's climbed with no other explanation, or dropping pressure throughout the whole house rather than at one fixture. We confirm the leak is actually in the service line, not somewhere inside the house, before recommending a repair.

Franklin's clay-heavy soil shifts seasonally, and a buried service line running through that moving ground is under more stress over time than a line in more stable soil. Older galvanized service lines, common in parts of the city's historic core, are especially prone to this kind of gradual failure, corroding and finally splitting at a weak point along the run.

Common Question

How Do I Know If It's My Line Or The City's?

This is one of the most common questions on these calls. In Franklin, the property owner is generally responsible for the line from the meter to the house, while the city maintains the line up to the meter. If your water bill is climbing and the meter itself is spinning with every fixture in the house off, the leak is almost always on your side of the meter. We confirm this before quoting any repair, since a leak on the city's side of the meter is a call to Franklin's water department, not a plumbing repair.

Local Context

Water Service Line Repair In Franklin, TN

This call comes up often enough in Franklin's older neighborhoods running original galvanized service lines that it's worth knowing the signs before the bill jumps too high. A soggy patch of yard between the meter and the house, paired with a bill that's climbed for no other reason, is usually enough to confirm the problem before a technician even arrives.

A meter that keeps spinning with every fixture in the house off is one of the clearest, easiest self-checks a Franklin homeowner can do before calling. Turn off every tap, appliance, and irrigation zone, then check the small dial or digital readout on the meter itself. If it's still moving, water is flowing somewhere between the meter and the house whether or not anything is visibly wet yet.

FAQs

Common Questions

Whose responsibility is a leak between the meter and my house?

Generally the homeowner's. The city maintains the line up to the meter. We confirm which side a leak is on before quoting.

Will my yard be torn up for this repair?

Trenchless methods, where the line and soil conditions allow, minimize this significantly compared to a full trench dig.

How is this different from a slab leak?

A service line leak is in the buried line before it reaches the house. A slab leak is in a supply line already under the house's foundation. The symptoms can look similar, which is why we confirm the actual location before quoting.

Can I keep using water while this gets fixed?

Often a temporary workaround is possible depending on the leak's severity, though a significant leak may require shutting off water to the property until repaired.
