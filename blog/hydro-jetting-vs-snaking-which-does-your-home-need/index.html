Hydro Jetting vs. Snaking: Which Does Your Franklin Home Actually Need?

By Franklin Express Plumbing · August 2026

These two services get lumped together as "drain clearing" often enough that a lot of homeowners assume they're interchangeable, just different price points for the same result. They're not. They solve overlapping but genuinely different problems, and picking the wrong one either wastes money or leaves the underlying issue unresolved.

What Snaking Actually Does

A mechanical snake, or auger, is a flexible cable fed into the pipe that physically breaks up and pulls back, or pushes through, whatever's blocking the line. It's fast, relatively inexpensive, and highly effective for a single, first-time clog: a foreign object, a localized buildup, a simple obstruction sitting in one spot. The limitation is that a snake clears a path through the blockage. It doesn't scour the surrounding pipe wall, so material left behind, particularly grease coating or root growth, is still there and can rebuild into another clog.

Snake sizing and technique also matter more than people assume. A small handheld auger meant for a bathroom sink trap is a very different tool than a motorized drum machine used on a main sewer line, and using the wrong scale of equipment either fails to clear the blockage or, in the case of an oversized tool on a delicate fixture line, risks damage to older, more brittle pipe. This is part of why a professional visit for a stubborn clog often succeeds where a store-bought drain snake didn't, it's not just persistence, it's matching the right tool to the actual line.

What Hydro Jetting Actually Does

Hydro jetting uses a high-pressure water stream to clean the entire interior circumference of the pipe, not just punch through the center. It removes grease coating, mineral scale, and root intrusion from the pipe walls themselves, which is why it tends to produce results that last considerably longer for the specific problems it's suited to. It's also more involved, and costs more, than a standard snaking visit.

The Actual Decision Framework

Budget is the primary constraint and it's a first occurrenceSnaking

These two services aren't always an either-or choice. On a line with heavy, longstanding buildup, some technicians will snake first to restore basic flow immediately, then follow with jetting to fully clear the pipe walls once flow is reestablished. Whether that combined approach makes sense for a specific line usually comes down to how severe the blockage is and how urgently flow needs to be restored before the more thorough service can be scheduled.

Why We Recommend A Camera First For Recurring Clogs

Before jumping straight to hydro jetting, we generally run a camera inspection on any line with a recurring-clog history. This confirms what's actually happening, root intrusion, grease buildup, a structural pipe defect, rather than assuming the more expensive service is automatically the right call. Sometimes a camera reveals the problem is a single, fixable pipe defect rather than something jetting alone would resolve.

What A Camera Inspection Actually Shows

A sewer camera is a waterproof, self-leveling camera fed through the line on a flexible cable, transmitting a live video feed a technician can review in real time. It reveals the specific condition of the pipe: whether roots are present and how extensive the growth is, whether there's a genuine structural defect like a crack, offset joint, or bellied section holding standing water, and roughly how much grease or scale coating has built up along the walls. This is meaningfully more useful than an educated guess based on symptoms alone, since two different underlying causes can produce very similar clogging patterns from the homeowner's side of the wall.

Quick Answers

Does a camera inspection cost extra on top of the service? We typically include a camera check as part of diagnosing a recurring-clog line rather than pricing it as a wholly separate line item, since it directly informs which service is actually needed.

Can jetting be done through a cleanout, or does it require digging? Most residential jetting is done through an existing cleanout access point without any excavation. Digging is only needed if no cleanout exists and one needs to be added.

Is one jetting service permanent? For grease buildup, results often last years with normal use. For active root intrusion, regrowth into the same opening is possible over time unless the underlying pipe defect is also addressed.

How do I know if my Franklin home even has a cleanout? Most homes built from the 1970s onward have one, typically a capped pipe near the foundation line. Older homes sometimes don't, in which case we can confirm access options during the visit.

Is Jetting Safe For Older Franklin Pipes?

For most cast-iron and clay lines in reasonable structural condition, yes, calibrated appropriately for the pipe material and confirmed by camera first. We won't jet a line that the camera shows is structurally compromised, since high pressure against a genuinely weak pipe wall isn't the right tool for that situation. This is exactly why the inspection step matters rather than skipping straight to a service based on a phone description alone.

The Honest Summary

Snaking is the right first move for most one-off clogs, and there's no reason to pay more for jetting on a problem a snake solves completely. Jetting earns its higher cost specifically on recurring, root-affected, or heavily built-up lines where a snake has already shown it isn't holding. If a drain in your home keeps coming back after being cleared, that repeat pattern is usually the clearest signal of which service actually fits.

The most useful thing a homeowner can do before calling either way is simply note the pattern honestly: is this the first time, or the third? That single detail, more than anything else, is what actually points toward the right service before a technician even arrives.

Cost-conscious homeowners sometimes worry that mentioning a recurring clog will automatically steer a visit toward the more expensive service. A straightforward diagnostic conversation, camera first where warranted, protects against that concern either way, since the equipment shows what's actually happening rather than leaving the decision to guesswork on either side.
