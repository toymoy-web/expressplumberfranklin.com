Why Does My Drain Keep Clogging? Common Culprits in Franklin Homes

By Franklin Express Plumbing · August 2026

Every drain clogs occasionally, that's more or less unavoidable with normal household use. What's worth paying attention to is a drain that clogs repeatedly, especially in the same spot, on a short cycle. That pattern is information, and it usually points to one of a handful of specific, identifiable causes rather than just bad luck.

The instinct when a drain clogs for the third or fourth time is often to just reach for the same fix that worked before, a plunger, a bottle of drain cleaner, a quick call for another snaking visit, without stopping to ask why it keeps happening in the first place. That repeated-fix cycle can go on for a surprisingly long time before anyone steps back and treats the pattern itself as the actual problem worth solving, rather than each individual clog.

Kitchen Sink: Grease And Food Buildup

The most common recurring culprit in a kitchen drain is grease that was poured down warm and liquid, then solidified somewhere along the pipe as it cooled, gradually narrowing the interior diameter with each additional pour. Coffee grounds and starchy food scraps compound the problem by sticking to that grease layer. The fix for a first-time clog is usually straightforward, but a kitchen line with a genuine history of grease buildup often benefits from more than a standard snake, since a snake punches through the buildup rather than removing it from the pipe walls.

Bathroom Drains: Hair And Soap Scum

Bathroom sink and tub drains clog primarily from hair combined with soap scum, which binds together into a mass that's surprisingly effective at trapping everything else that goes down the drain afterward. A simple drain screen prevents most of this before it starts, and it's one of the lowest-effort fixes available for a genuinely common problem.

Main Line: Tree Roots In Older Neighborhoods

If multiple drains throughout the house are backing up around the same time, rather than just one isolated fixture, that points toward the main sewer line rather than an individual drain. In Franklin's older neighborhoods with mature trees and aging clay or cast-iron sewer laterals, tree root intrusion is a genuinely common cause of this pattern. Roots exploit small cracks or joint separations, often caused by the same clay-soil movement that affects foundations throughout the area, and grow into the pipe seeking moisture and nutrients.

Floor Drains And Laundry Lines: The Overlooked Culprits

Kitchen and bathroom drains get most of the attention, but floor drains in basements or utility rooms and washing machine discharge lines clog for reasons that are easy to overlook. Lint from laundry accumulates in a discharge line over years the same way hair accumulates in a bathroom drain, and a rarely used floor drain can develop a buildup simply from infrequent flow keeping debris from ever fully clearing. If a basement floor drain has started backing up during heavy laundry days or after a load with a lot of lint-producing fabric, that's a specific, identifiable pattern worth mentioning when describing the issue, since it points toward a different section of line than a kitchen or bathroom complaint would.

Quick Answers

Are store-bought drain cleaners safe to use regularly? Occasional use on a minor clog is generally fine, but regular reliance on chemical drain cleaners can actually damage older pipes over time, particularly aging cast-iron lines common in Franklin's historic core.

Why does my drain gurgle sometimes but not clog? Gurgling without a full clog often indicates a partial obstruction or a venting issue that hasn't fully blocked flow yet, worth having checked before it progresses to an actual backup.

Is it normal for a garbage disposal to make drain clogs worse? Yes, in some cases. A disposal that isn't fully breaking down food waste can send larger particles further into the line than they'd otherwise travel, contributing to buildup further down.

Should I be worried if a new house I bought has a drain that clogs occasionally? Occasional, unrelated clogs across different fixtures are normal wear and tear. It's specifically the repeated, same-drain pattern within weeks that's worth having actually looked at rather than treated as routine.

  • One fixture, recurring: Usually localized buildup specific to that drain, grease in a kitchen line or hair in a bathroom line.
  • Multiple fixtures, around the same time: Points toward the main line, worth a camera inspection rather than repeated individual snaking.
  • Gurgling in unrelated drains: A sign of a partial obstruction affecting venting somewhere in the system, often in the main line.
  • Slow drainage that's gradually gotten worse over months: Consistent with a growing buildup, whether grease, scale, or root intrusion, rather than a sudden single-event clog.

When A Standard Clearing Isn't Enough

A first-time clog usually responds well to standard mechanical drain cleaning. A recurring clog in the same location is where it's worth stepping back and asking why it keeps happening rather than just clearing it again. For lines with confirmed grease buildup or root intrusion, hydro jetting clears the full interior diameter of the pipe rather than just punching a path through the obstruction, which is why it tends to hold longer for exactly these recurring cases.

A Practical Habit That Helps

Keeping a rough mental note of which drains clog and how often is more useful than it sounds. A pattern that seemed random in the moment often makes a lot more sense once you can see it laid out: "the kitchen sink every few months" is a different problem than "every drain in the house, twice this year," and knowing which one you're actually dealing with is most of the diagnostic work already done before anyone even shows up.

That small bit of pattern-tracking is often the difference between a quick, correctly targeted fix and a series of repeat visits addressing the same symptom without ever getting to the actual cause underneath it.

Whatever the specific cause turns out to be, a recurring clog is rarely something that resolves on its own with enough patience. It's worth treating the second or third occurrence as the signal to actually get it looked at, rather than the fifth or sixth.
