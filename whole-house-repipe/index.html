Whole House Repipe in Franklin, TN

Franklin's historic core has genuine pre-1960s housing stock still running original galvanized supply lines in some cases, and neighborhoods built in the 1980s occasionally have polybutylene, both of which are past their realistic service life and prone to internal corrosion or joint failure. A whole house repipe replaces the supply lines with PEX or copper, and we plan the route to minimize how much drywall needs to come down.

Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, which means a homeowner often doesn't see the problem until water pressure has already dropped noticeably or a pinhole leak shows up inside a wall. Polybutylene, common in some 1980s and early 1990s construction nationally, is prone to joint and fitting failure as it ages. Both situations, common enough in Franklin's older housing stock, eventually point toward a full repipe rather than a series of individual patch repairs that keep recurring.

Common Question

How Do I Know If My Franklin Home Needs A Repipe?

The clearest sign isn't a single leak, it's a pattern. One pinhole leak in an old galvanized line is a repair. A second unrelated leak a year later, plus dropping water pressure across multiple fixtures, means the pipe material itself has reached the end of its service life and further individual repairs are just delaying the same outcome. For homes with confirmed polybutylene, insurance carriers and inspectors often flag it regardless of current symptoms, which is worth knowing before it becomes a sale or refinance issue.

FAQs

Common Questions

Is PEX or copper better for a repipe?

PEX is more flexible, less prone to freeze damage, and generally lower cost installed. Copper has a longer track record and some homeowners prefer it for that reason. We present both honestly.

How much wall damage does a repipe cause?

We plan routing to minimize openings, but some drywall access is unavoidable. We leave clean, patch-ready cuts.

Do I need a repipe if I only have one leak?

Not necessarily. One isolated leak is usually a repair. A repipe becomes the right call when leaks recur or pressure drops system-wide.

Does polybutylene pipe need to be replaced even without leaks?

Many insurers and inspectors treat confirmed poly-B as a liability regardless of current condition, since failure is considered a matter of when, not if.
