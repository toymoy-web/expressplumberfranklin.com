Hydro Jetting in Franklin, TN

When a standard snake keeps clearing a clog only for it to come back weeks later, that's usually a sign the line needs more than mechanical clearing. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the full interior diameter of a pipe, removing grease buildup, scale, and root intrusion that a snake pushes through but doesn't actually clear.

Franklin's older neighborhoods with mature trees and aging clay or cast-iron sewer lines are where hydro jetting comes up most, since root intrusion tends to spread along the full interior surface of a pipe rather than sitting in one clog point. A snake can punch a hole through root mass and restore flow temporarily, but the roots regrow into the same opening within months. Jetting clears the full pipe wall, which is why it holds longer.

Common Question

Do I Need Hydro Jetting Or Is Snaking Enough?

Most single, first-time clogs are fine with a standard snake, it's faster and less expensive. Jetting becomes the right call when a line has recurring clogs, confirmed root intrusion, or heavy grease buildup along the pipe walls that a snake keeps pushing through rather than removing. We generally run a camera first on a recurring-clog line so a homeowner can see exactly what's happening before paying for the more involved service.

FAQs

Common Questions

Is hydro jetting safe for older pipes?

For most cast-iron and clay lines in good structural condition, yes. We check pipe condition via camera first and won't jet a line that's structurally compromised.

How often does a jetted line need to be redone?

It varies by cause. A line with active root intrusion may need jetting again in a year or two unless the root source is addressed. Grease buildup lines can go much longer.

Is jetting more expensive than snaking?

Yes, but it typically lasts longer for recurring or root-affected lines, which can make it the better value over time.

Can jetting damage my pipes?

Properly calibrated pressure for the pipe material and condition, confirmed by camera first, is safe for the vast majority of residential lines.
