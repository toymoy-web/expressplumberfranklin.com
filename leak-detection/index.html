Franklin's karst-clay soil shifts slightly with seasonal moisture changes, and that movement puts stress on pipes running beneath a slab foundation, especially in homes with copper supply lines that have been in the ground for decades. A slab leak often starts small and worsens gradually, which is why the water bill and a warm or damp floor spot are usually the first real signs, well before any visible water shows up.

Common Question

How Do I Know If It's A Slab Leak?

The combination that points most clearly to a slab leak is a water bill that's climbed without a clear reason, plus a specific warm or damp spot on the floor, especially over a hot water line run. Not every unexplained water loss is a slab leak, sometimes it's a running toilet or an irrigation line, so we check the easy explanations first. When those don't account for it, electronic leak detection locates the exact point before any demolition, which keeps the eventual repair contained to a small, precise area instead of an exploratory cut across the slab.

FAQs

Common Questions

How accurate is electronic leak detection?

Modern acoustic and pressure equipment typically narrows a leak location to within a small, targeted area, which is what keeps repair demolition minimal.

Is a slab leak an emergency?

It's rarely a sudden emergency, but it worsens over time and can eventually affect the foundation. It's worth addressing promptly once suspected.

Will my homeowner's insurance cover slab leak repair?

Coverage varies by policy. Sudden leaks are often covered, gradual ones sometimes are not. We recommend checking your specific policy.

Do you repair the leak too, or just find it?

We do both, detection and repair, with the repair quoted precisely once the leak location is confirmed.
