Repair Or Replace

Is It Worth Fixing An Older Disposal

Repair Makes Sense

  • Unit is under 6-8 years old
  • The issue is a jam or a worn seal
  • It's the first failure, not a pattern
  • Motor still runs, just isn't spinning freely

Replacement Makes Sense

  • Unit is past 8-10 years old
  • Motor has burned out or won't reset
  • This is the second or third repair visit
  • Repair cost approaches half of a new unit

The reset button on the underside of most disposals is worth checking before assuming the worst. A tripped reset from an overheated motor is common and simply needs the unit to cool down before it can be reset and restarted. If the reset button won't hold or trips again immediately after restart, that's usually a sign of a deeper electrical or motor issue worth an actual diagnosis.

FAQs

Common Questions

Why does my disposal just hum?

That usually means the motor is receiving power but the flywheel is jammed. Never reach in by hand, a repair visit clears this safely.

Is it worth repairing an old disposal?

If it's a simple jam or seal issue, yes. If the motor has failed and the unit is past 8-10 years old, replacement is usually the better value.

Can I run anything through a disposal?

Fibrous items like celery, grease, and bones cause most jams and premature failures. Sticking to soft food scraps extends its life.

Do you install customer-supplied units?

In most cases, yes, though we'll confirm compatibility with existing mounting hardware first.
