Sump Pump Installation & Repair in Franklin, TN

A sump pump that fails during a heavy Middle Tennessee rain event is one of the most common ways a Franklin basement floods, and it usually happens at the worst possible time, mid-storm, when the pump is working hardest. We repair failed pumps same-day and, for homes that have outgrown their existing pump or never had one sized correctly, install systems matched to how much water Franklin's karst-clay soil actually pushes toward a foundation during peak rainfall.

Williamson County's spring rain season, typically March through May, is when sump pumps see their heaviest demand, with a secondary spike during summer thunderstorms. A pump that was adequate when a home was built may be undersized for how much groundwater a property actually sees now, especially if grading or drainage elsewhere on the lot has changed. Battery backup matters more here than in a lot of markets, because the same severe weather that triggers heavy groundwater intrusion is also what's most likely to knock out power.

Common Question

How Do I Know If My Sump Pump Is Actually Sized Right?

A lot of Franklin homeowners only find out their sump pump is undersized after it fails during the storm that mattered most. The honest way to check is to look at how often the pump is cycling during a normal heavy rain, if it's running near-constantly rather than cycling on and off, it's either undersized for current inflow or nearing failure. We measure basin size and estimate peak inflow based on the property's grading and known karst-soil water table behavior, then size a replacement pump with enough headroom for a genuinely heavy storm, not just an average one.

A property near the Harpeth River floodplain or on a lot with known drainage issues needs more pump capacity and a backup system than a well-graded lot on higher ground, even if both homes are the same age and size.

FAQs

Common Questions

How often should a sump pump be replaced?

Most residential pumps last 7-10 years under normal use. Homes with heavier inflow from karst-soil conditions may see shorter lifespans.

Is a battery backup necessary?

In Franklin, where heavy rain and power outages often happen together, a backup system meaningfully reduces flood risk during the storms that matter most.

Why does my pump run even when it's not raining?

Continuous groundwater seepage in clay-heavy soil can keep a basin filling slowly even between storms. Constant cycling is worth having checked.

Can a sump pump be paired with basement waterproofing?

Yes, and in most karst-soil cases it should be. Drain tile collects the water, the pump moves it out.
